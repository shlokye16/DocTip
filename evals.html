Clinician Reasoning Check · At a glance

A small pause.
A clearer reason for the plan.

Our demo helps clinicians revisit assumptions and explain their reasoning. Here is what it looks for across seven conditions.

Synthetic demo · Not for clinical use

How it works

01 · DURING THE VISIT

A quiet reminder

With consent, the browser transcribes the conversation. A specific concern in the current conversation can trigger a short tip and soft beep, up to three tips per visit.

02 · AFTER THE VISIT

Two quick choices

Select what guided the assessment and whether the plan would stay the same for someone with the same findings and a different background.

03 · AT THE END

A reasoning note

Save both selections in the encounter note. The checklist records the clinician's reflection without scoring their choices.

From an assumption to a question

These are illustrative reasoning patterns, not descriptions of every clinician or patient. The app asks for an explanation; it does not decide whether a diagnosis or care plan is correct.

Seven profiles, one goal: keep the patient's story and relevant findings visible.

The same consistency check applies across profiles: would the reasoning stay the same if demographic details changed while the clinically relevant information stayed the same?

What the evidence means

Working software is different from proof of better clinical care.

10 of 12 written answers classified correctly

In the earlier free-text test, the judge agreed with the expected rating on 4 of 4 chest-pain answers, 3 of 4 sickle-cell answers, and 3 of 4 preeclampsia answers. Both errors were specific answers marked insufficient. The other four conditions have no comparable judge scores. This benchmark predates the checklist.

20.1 percentage points → 0

In a separate toy chest-pain model, changing only sex or insurance changed the predicted urgency score. Across four synthetic populations, the average of the larger of those two gaps fell from 20.1 points to 0 after editing the model. Accuracy against the synthetic labels rose from 86.4% to 98.7%. Patient outcomes and clinician bias were outside this experiment.

A reflection aid, not a medical decision. Profile wording is draft material requiring clinician review. The app does not diagnose, recommend treatment, or reliably distinguish speakers. It tracks recent context and can miss a correction, a speaker change, or an unfamiliar phrase. Use synthetic data only.
